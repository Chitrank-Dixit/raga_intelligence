## Prompt 5 — Improve pitch tracking and voiced-frame detection



**Files to inspect:**

- `src/raga_intelligence/features/pitch.py`

- `src/raga_intelligence/features/extractor.py`



**Problem**



The current implementation uses `librosa.yin()` and treats pitch estimates within the configured frequency range as voiced frames.



YIN can produce estimates for unvoiced or noisy frames. This can contaminate pitch-class distributions, voiced ratios, and downstream predictions.



**Implementation requirements**



1. Evaluate replacing the current voiced-frame logic with `librosa.pyin()` or another suitable pitch tracker that provides voicing information.

2. Separate pitch estimation from voiced/unvoiced classification.

3. Use voicing probability or confidence where available.

4. Make pitch extraction parameters configurable.

5. Handle silence, percussion, noise, and out-of-range frequencies safely.

6. Preserve a consistent output format for downstream feature extraction.

7. Compare the new approach against the existing YIN implementation before removing the old one.



**Tests**



- Synthetic sine waves at known frequencies.

- Silence.

- White noise.

- Unvoiced/percussive audio.

- Audio with changing fundamental frequency.

- Audio containing pitch values outside the configured range.



**Acceptance criteria**



Voiced-frame statistics must not treat every plausible YIN frequency estimate as a confirmed voiced frame.

## Prompt 6 — Improve tonic estimation



**Files to inspect:**

- `src/raga_intelligence/features/tonic.py`

- `src/raga_intelligence/features/pitch.py`

- `src/raga_intelligence/features/extractor.py`



**Problem**



The current tonic estimator appears to select the strongest chroma pitch class using a Sa + Pa + Ma combination and then map it to a fixed MIDI octave, clamped between 100 and 300 Hz.



The strongest pitch class is not necessarily the tonic. This can produce incorrect tonic estimates, especially with accompaniment, harmonically dense audio, or Western chord-based recordings.



**Implementation requirements**



1. Inspect the current tonic estimation algorithm and document its assumptions.

2. Investigate a more robust method based on:

   - Stable pitch-class occurrence.

   - Harmonic salience.

   - Drone or sustained-note detection.

   - Temporal consistency.

3. Support multiple tonic candidates when confidence is low.

4. Return an explicit confidence or uncertainty measure.

5. Preserve the ability to supply a known tonic for controlled experiments.

6. Do not assume a fixed octave is the correct tonic octave.

7. Avoid silently treating a low-confidence estimate as ground truth.



**Tests**



- Synthetic drone recordings with known tonic.

- Recordings with tonic and Pa emphasis.

- Recordings with strong accompaniment.

- Recordings with changing melodic emphasis.

- Recordings with ambiguous tonic evidence.



**Acceptance criteria**



Tonic estimation must expose uncertainty and must not automatically equate the strongest chroma bin with the tonic.
